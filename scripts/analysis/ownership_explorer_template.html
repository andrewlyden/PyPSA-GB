How the cases are organised

Start with the scenario, which is its proposition, then its propositions family. Everything below them changes one assumption at a time, unless it is explicitly described as a named package or a bound.

Policy propositionsCoherent packages a government could legislate, where the levers are set as consequences of one another rather than independently. Each public scenario is its headline proposition (P1, P2); the cases listed here are the rest of the family — the fiscal alternative, the wires, full nationalisation and the declared financing stresses.
Structural leversContract scope and design: existing CfDs, strike setting, amortisation, route booking, merchant contracting and the LDES floor.
Workbook alternativesFinancing assumptions taken from the supporting workbook: private WACC, public borrowing basis, construction premium and sovereign yields.
Ownership optionsChanges to the asset boundary or market institution, including legacy assets, networks, CfD vintage and wholesale pricing.
Named positionsOther multi-lever positions taken from the workplan or the supporting workbook, such as a publicly owned company, rather than isolated one-variable tests. They also hold the conservative readings each public scenario had before the propositions were promoted into it.
BoundsDeliberately combined alternatives. These show an outer comparison and should not be read as a central forecast.

Reading these numbers

One physical run, several bills. Dispatch is the same in every case; ownership changes who is paid what, at which cost of capital. The private scenario reproduces the CP30 2030 forecast bill on every shared line.

Comparators. A sensitivity that also moves the private baseline (the WACC basis, merchant new build at cost, the storage floor, the pay-as-bid bound) is measured against the private case at the same point, named on the page. Everything else is measured against the central private bill.

Two bills for the state-owned cases. Cases that keep the market are projected through the live bill projector with re-priced CfD strikes and the public-account lines. Cases that buy the legacy fleet replace the market lines with the cost of the owned plant (the acquisition tariff); their purchase price is always shown beside the saving.

Bounds, not cases. The pay-as-bid ceiling is the most a pay-as-bid settlement could remove and rests on assumptions the model does not make; the "all six levers" bound is every structural lever at once.

Vintage. The twelve solved months carry the contract-value balancing bid stack, so each CfD unit bids what its contract is actually worth. What is still held fixed is the constraint line across ownership cases: a public owner would bid at cost where a CfD unit bids strike less reference, and that is not yet solved. The omission runs one way only, so every saving here is a lower bound on that count, by up to about GBP 16 a household.
